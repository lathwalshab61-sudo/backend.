Evolution of Fingerprint Science

Journey through the landmark cases that defined modern forensic identification. Explore the historical context and review simulated analyses of early dermatoglyphic evidence.

1892

The Rojas Homicide

Inspector Eduardo Álvarez made the first criminal fingerprint identification. He matched a bloody mark on a doorpost to Francisca Rojas, disproving her initial claims and revolutionizing criminal investigations.

1903

The Will West Case

Two inmates at Leavenworth Penitentiary shared identical names and Bertillon measurements. Fingerprints were the only way to distinguish them, marking the end of anthropometry as the primary identification method.

1910

Thomas Jennings Case

The first murder conviction in the United States based primarily on fingerprint evidence. A latent print left in fresh paint became the cornerstone of forensic admissibility in US courts.
